# Misuse of protective laws

Protective laws are sometimes misused: false or exaggerated cruelty/dowry complaints (BNS 85/86, formerly IPC 498A), false sexual-harassment or "false promise to marry" complaints (BNS 69). The accused — often with elderly parents — faces arrest, stigma and years of litigation, with no more legal knowledge than a victim has.

## What the product does
- **First 48 hours:** rights on police contact — notice of appearance instead of automatic arrest for offences under 7 years (BNSS 35, *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar*); anticipatory bail (BNSS 482). See [[bns-bnss-bsa]].
- **Defence building:** preserve chats, call logs, locations, witnesses; timeline; flag contradictions ([[opposition-analyst]]).
- **Routes:** quashing (BNSS 528), mediation/settlement, and after acquittal, action for false charge (BNS 248) or defamation.
- Same [[strategy-loop]] at every hearing ([[criminal-courts]]).

Guardrails apply both ways: [[hard-lines]]; honest strength assessment. Persona: [[persona-accused-respondent]]. Decision: [[adr-001-serve-any-side]].
